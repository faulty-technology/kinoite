# radiance at 0.57 and 131K, and two subagents at a time on the client

## Decision

`radiance.container` serves with `--gpu-memory-utilization 0.57`, no
`--kv-cache-memory` pin (vLLM profiles the pool), and `--max-model-len 131072`,
in place of upstream's 0.98, the measured 18,563,072,000-byte pin and 262144.

Paired with two client settings in the laptop's `~/.pi/agent`: the radiance
model's `contextWindow` is 131072 in `models.json`, so the client compacts before
it can send a prompt the server would reject, and `MAX_CONCURRENCY = 2` in
`extensions/subagent/index.ts`.

The client cap in
[decisions/2026-09-19-four-slots-and-a-client-concurrency-cap](2026-09-19-four-slots-and-a-client-concurrency-cap.md)
was sized for lemonade's slots and does not carry over to radiance; this sets
the cap for radiance. The lemonade half of that decision is untouched.

## Why

**The point is free VRAM.** At the upstream settings radiance claims 32.5 of
32.6 GiB per card and nothing else can load. At 0.57 and 131072 the whole stack
fits in 18.0 GiB per card and leaves about 13.8 GiB free on each
([runs/2026-09-25-radiance-dfdfa38-and-cold-start-floor](../runs/2026-09-25-radiance-dfdfa38-and-cold-start-floor.md)).
That is room for a small second model beside it, such as a Jev-style decision
model, which the upstream settings rule out.

**The pool it gives up was idle.** The pinned pool holds 943,581 tokens, 3.60x
a full 262,144-token request. At 0.57 it holds 165,906, 1.27x one 131,072-token
request (same run). At 0.55 decode was within 1% of the pinned configuration at
every depth measured
([runs/2026-09-15-radiance-quadlet-floor](../runs/2026-09-15-radiance-quadlet-floor.md)):
cache size does not buy decode on this engine.

**Serializing deep work costs no throughput.** At 45K and 150K per stream, N
concurrent streams take N times as long as one: speedup 0.99–1.02x from N = 2 to
8 ([runs/2026-09-20-radiance-concurrency-at-depth](../runs/2026-09-20-radiance-concurrency-at-depth.md)).
The work is prefill-bound and one stream already saturates the cards. So a pool
that fits about one deep conversation, and a client capped at two subagents,
costs latency shape on shallow tasks and nothing on deep ones. Past the pool,
vLLM preempts and recomputes rather than failing.

**The three settings have to move together.** The server rejects prompts past
131,072 with an HTTP 400, so a client window above it turns compaction into an
error. And the pool is sized for one deep conversation, so a client fanning out
four deep subagents would spend its time preempting.

## Rejected

**Upstream's 0.98 with the pin and 262144.** Measured and shipped until now. It
fills both cards to within ~100 MiB for a pool mostly never used.

**0.55, the measured floor for a 131K conversation.** It fails a start from an
empty compile cache, which every update causes: 2.69 GiB of KV against the 2.79
needed for one 131,072-token request. It serves only after `Restart=always` retries
it warm
([runs/2026-09-25-radiance-dfdfa38-and-cold-start-floor](../runs/2026-09-25-radiance-dfdfa38-and-cold-start-floor.md)).
0.57 is the lowest ceiling that started cold, and 0.56 was not tried.

**A middle ceiling sized for two 131K conversations, around 0.62.** Estimated,
never measured, and the concurrency result above says the second deep
conversation buys no throughput.

**Keeping the client at 262144 against a 131072 server.** Every session past
131K would end in a 400 instead of a compaction.

**Keeping `MAX_CONCURRENCY = 4`.** Four deep subagents against a ~166K pool is
continuous preemption for no throughput gain.

## Unverified

How often real sessions preempt at this pool size is unmeasured. The
`vllm:num_preemptions_total` counter on `:8005/metrics` is where to look. How
much compacting at 131K instead of 262K costs the work itself is also unmeasured.
The user is judging that over the next few sessions. No second model has been
chosen or loaded beside radiance yet, so the free 13.8 GiB has not been exercised.
